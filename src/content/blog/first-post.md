A new store is a blank canvas with defaults that quietly shape everything you do next. Most setup guides are catalogues of every screen in the admin. This one is the short version: the settings that matter on day one.

## Pick a theme you can live with

The theme decides your layout constraints, your typography, and how much CSS you inherit. Spend the time here before you install anything else. A theme you fight every week is a tax you pay forever.

Start with a theme whose structure you understand. Online Store 2.0 themes with configurable sections are easier to reason about than a theme that hides everything behind a page builder.

## Set the navigation before you add products

Navigation is information architecture, not a menu editor. A store with three collections and a nested dropdown has already lost the visitor. Decide what the first click on the home page should do, then make that the first link.

A useful default for a small store:

- Home
- Shop (the collection view)
- About
- Contact

Four items. Anything more is a question, not a navigation.

## Change the defaults that shape trust

Three settings read as signals even when nobody is looking at the admin:

- Set a real domain and make sure the storefront is reachable on HTTPS.
- Write an About page in your own voice. It is the page most new visitors check.
- Turn off the marketing app banners and countdown timers that ship with some themes. They add urgency the store cannot back up yet.

## Add apps only when the gap hurts

Wait for the store to feel slow before adding an app. A store with three apps does less, costs less, and breaks less than a store with twenty. Most "must have" lists are someone else's margin.

The setup that survives contact with reality is the setup you can explain in one paragraph. If the store needs a paragraph per app, cut apps until it does not.
