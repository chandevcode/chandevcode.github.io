Most product pages are written by the supplier, not for the buyer. The result is a wall of specifications and a buy button that nobody trusts. The fix is structure, not copy that shouts.

## Answer the question the buyer is actually asking

Every product page answers one question: will this solve my problem? The page should answer it in the first screen. Name the outcome in the headline, show the product doing it, and say how it works in plain words.

If the page cannot answer in one sentence, the product is unclear, and no amount of layout will fix that.

## Price it like the decision it is

The price should be visible, stable, and near the buy button. Present it once and keep the same number in the cart, taxes, and at checkout. A price that changes between pages is how trust dies.

If a discount exists, make it a real number on the product page, not a countdown. Urgency you cannot back up reads as desperation.

## Remove friction before you add persuasion

Ask where a buyer can lose the purchase. A page that forces a signup, hides shipping, or drops to a blank checkout page loses more sales than any headline wins.

The shortest version: keep the buyer on the page, say what happens next, and make checkout as short as the law allows.

## Checkout you can test in one evening

Order anything from your own store, on your own phone, and write down every place you hesitated. Those are your friction points. Fix them in the order they appear, not in the order the theme lists its settings.
