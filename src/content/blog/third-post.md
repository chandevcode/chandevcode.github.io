Running a store alone means every app subscription is a bet against your margins. This is a small stack, not a shopping list: enough to cover the work that otherwise owns your evenings.

## Support and inbox

A single shared inbox beats five tools with five notification settings. When a customer writes, the answer should be the same wherever the message lands. Pick one channel, answer it yourself, and resist the ticket system until the volume actually hurts.

## Shipping and returns

The shipping app that produces labels from the same screen as the order saves more minutes than any dashboard metric. And returns are a feature, not a failure: make the return form reachable from the order email, and the customer stays a customer.

## The boring spreadsheet

Before adding any analytics app, keep a plain spreadsheet of orders, costs, and the questions customers ask. After a month, the questions column is your roadmap. Most "insights" tools tell you what you already know; the questions column tells you what you do not.

## Cut until the stack fits in one paragraph

Review the subscription list every quarter. If an app needs a second explanation to justify itself, it has already lost. The stack that pays for itself is the stack you forget is installed.
